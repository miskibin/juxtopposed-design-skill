# Running a critique / redesign

## Method

1. **Use the product as its users do.** Walk the most common journeys. Count the steps for the most frequent actions. Note where you hesitate.
2. **Map the screen to intentions.** For each major area: whose interest does it serve — the user's goal, or a business metric? What is competing with the main content?
3. **Diagnose with principles.** Every problem you name should map to a principle (attention, friction, context separation, ergonomics, system, color hierarchy, typography, restraint, calm, feedback, medium fit). Unnamed problems are just taste.
4. **Keep what works.** Say explicitly what the current design does well; a redesign that throws away good decisions is not an upscale.
5. **Redesign the structure first** (flow, hierarchy, zones), then the system, then the surface.
6. **Explain trade-offs.** If the business needs something the redesign de-emphasises (ads, upsells), show where it can live without harming the main goal.

Output shape: **Observe → Oppose → Upscale**, with concrete references to screens and elements.

## Case studies

These illustrate the principles; they are not templates to copy.

### YouTube — protect attention
- **Observed:** the watch page is crowded by a recommendation grid; comments are pushed into a narrow side column; short-form content is injected into the long-form flow; the layout encourages compulsive scrolling and treats attention as something to extract.
- **Redesign move:** the video becomes the central focus; discussion regains a wide, readable space below it; recommendations move into modular, collapsible zones.
- **Principle:** the space for reflection and discussion must not compete with the algorithmic stream.

### Spotify — reduce friction
- **Observed:** a fragmented library; basic playlist operations buried in nested menus; podcasts blurring the music catalogue.
- **Redesign move:** a clear, unified library; direct contextual filters; the most-used controls brought to the surface; the player becomes a real control center with a clear hierarchy of information.
- **Principle:** the most frequent actions need the fewest intermediate steps.

### Discord — emotional ergonomics
- **Observed:** a cold, technical aesthetic; many vertical panels at once; aggressive notifications; an intimidating entry point for non-technical people.
- **Redesign move:** softer geometry, warmer dark tones, more breathing room, fewer simultaneous stimuli.
- **Principle:** social spaces should build comfort, not overstimulation.

### Nothing OS — restraint with style
- **Observed:** a signature dot-matrix typeface applied across the whole system, including clocks, timers and settings, slowing down reading.
- **Redesign move:** the signature reserved for a few expressive moments; system information carried by a neutral, instantly readable typeface.
- **Principle:** identity must never cost the legibility of critical functions.

### Steam — separate contexts
- **Observed:** store and personal library mixed together on an aging structure; weak typography; confusing navigation.
- **Redesign move:** a clear separation between the shop and what the user owns; a modern layout suited to wide screens.
- **Principle:** the place where people manage what they own is not a sales surface.

### Apple Music — fit the medium
- **Observed:** rigid catalogue structure; lyrics hard to reach; no connection between the interface's mood and the music.
- **Redesign move:** fluid transitions; backgrounds that take on the album's palette; synced lyrics at the center.
- **Principle:** a media interface can adopt the mood of what it presents.

## Generalising

Any product can be read through the same lenses: What is the main thing (attention)? What is done
most often (friction)? What is owned versus sold (contexts)? How is it held and used (ergonomics)?
What is its signature, and where does it get in the way (restraint)? How does it feel to spend an
hour in it (calm)? What medium does it carry (fit)?
